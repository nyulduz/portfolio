Critical Reflection Narrative

Section 1 Reflection: What, So What, Now What

1 What? (Description of the Experience & Creative Process)

For my Section 1 artifact, I selected my WebQuest project, titled Job Hunting Abroad. This project involved designing a complete WebQuest with learning activities and educational resources focused on exploring international employment opportunities. The purpose was to create an engaging, inquiry-based learning experience that connects educational content with a practical, real-world challenge.

The design process required me to organize the topic into meaningful learning activities and identify relevant resources that could guide learners through the exploration of international job opportunities. Rather than presenting information as a traditional lecture or simple reading assignment, I developed a structured learning experience that encourages learners to investigate information, explore possibilities, and develop a deeper understanding of the topic.

The WebQuest approach is grounded in inquiry-oriented learning. Dodge (1995) introduced WebQuests as activities in which learners use Internet-based resources to investigate questions and complete meaningful tasks. March (2004) further explained that effective WebQuests connect authentic tasks with online resources to promote critical thinking. This approach also supports active knowledge construction, allowing learners to engage with information rather than simply receive it (O'Bannon, 2000).

Creativity played an important role in developing this project. Runco and Jaeger (2012) explain that creativity involves both originality and usefulness. In this project, I applied that understanding by developing a learning experience around a practical topic that learners could connect to real-life career decisions. The project also reflects design-oriented thinking, which encourages educators to approach instructional challenges through flexible and creative processes (Henriksen et al., 2017).

2 So What? (Theoretical Significance & Growth as an Innovator)

This project is significant because it helped me understand that creativity in education is not limited to producing attractive materials or presenting information in different formats. Creativity involves designing meaningful learning experiences that address authentic needs and encourage learners to think independently.

The WebQuest demonstrates my progress toward MA EDIN Program Learning Outcome 1: promoting and cultivating creativity and innovative thinking for learning, service, and leadership. By selecting an authentic topic and developing structured activities and resources, I created an opportunity for learners to connect academic learning with practical challenges. Creativity is especially valuable when educational experiences encourage learners to develop original and useful responses to real-world situations (Henriksen et al., 2017; Runco & Jaeger, 2012).

The project also connects to inquiry-based learning. Dodge (1995) and March (2004) emphasize the importance of guiding learners toward meaningful tasks through carefully selected resources. Inquiry-oriented instruction encourages learners to investigate information, develop questions, and construct their own understanding. Bell et al. (2005) explain that inquiry activities can be designed at different levels depending on how much responsibility learners have for asking questions and developing explanations. Furthermore, Liang and Fung (2020) demonstrate that WebQuest-based instruction fosters exploratory talk and critical thinking among students.

Furthermore, the WebQuest reflects principles of constructivist learning, in which learners actively develop knowledge through engagement with information and meaningful tasks. Hmelo-Silver (2004) explains that problem-centered learning can support flexible knowledge, problem-solving, and self-directed learning. Similarly, Anderson and Krathwohl (2001) emphasize the importance of higher-order cognitive processes, including analyzing, evaluating, and creating. These processes are relevant to a learning activity that asks learners to explore employment information and consider possible career opportunities.

The project also connects with the ISTE Educator Standards, particularly the Designer competency (Standard 2.5), because it involves developing authentic learning activities that support meaningful learner engagement (International Society for Technology in Education [ISTE], n.d.). The OECD Learning Compass 2030 also emphasizes the importance of creating new value, taking responsibility, and developing competencies that prepare learners to navigate unfamiliar situations (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development [OECD], 2019).

As an education administrator and leader, this experience helped me recognize the importance of moving beyond traditional instructional approaches. Educational innovation requires understanding learners' needs, identifying meaningful challenges, and creating opportunities for active participation. Henriksen and Richardson (2017) emphasize that educators can approach educational problems as designers who use creative thinking to develop practical solutions. This perspective strengthened my understanding of how instructional creativity can contribute to improved educational experiences.

Overall, this project demonstrates my growing ability to connect creativity, inquiry, and instructional design. It helped me recognize that innovative education should not simply deliver information; it should encourage learners to explore, question, analyze, and apply knowledge to situations beyond the classroom.

3 Now What? (Future Application in Teaching & Leadership)

The experience of designing the WebQuest will influence how I approach future educational planning, instructional development, and organizational leadership. I intend to apply inquiry-based and learner-centered strategies when developing educational activities and supporting teachers in creating more engaging learning environments.

First, I will continue to incorporate authentic, real-world topics into instructional activities. Learners benefit when they can connect academic concepts to practical situations, explore relevant resources, and apply knowledge to meaningful challenges. Future instructional projects will therefore include opportunities for investigation, critical thinking, reflection, and problem-solving.

Second, I will encourage educators and instructional teams to approach curriculum development creatively. Design thinking can help educators identify learner needs, reconsider traditional approaches, and develop more effective learning experiences (Henriksen et al., 2017). As an education leader, I can support this process by encouraging collaboration, sharing instructional resources, and creating opportunities for teachers to experiment with new approaches.

Third, I will use the OECD Learning Compass 2030 as a reference when considering how educational activities can develop learner agency, creativity, and responsibility (OECD, 2019). I want learners to become more independent, confident, and capable of making informed decisions in unfamiliar situations.

Finally, I recognize that designing an instructional activity is only one part of educational innovation. It is also important to evaluate whether the learning experience achieves its intended goals. In future projects, I will pay greater attention to learner feedback, assessment criteria, and opportunities for continuous improvement. This approach will help me make instructional decisions based on evidence rather than assumptions.

Through this project, I have developed a stronger appreciation for creativity as a practical component of educational leadership and instructional design. My goal is to continue creating learning opportunities that connect knowledge with real-world challenges, encourage independent thinking, and prepare learners to participate confidently in an increasingly interconnected world.

Critical Reflection Narrative

Section 2 Reflection: What, So What, Now What

1 What? (Instructional Design & Technological Integration)

For this section of my ePortfolio, I selected a Transformative Learning Interview that explores how meaningful experiences can change an educator's beliefs, professional identity, and instructional practices. The interview focuses on a teacher's experience during her first year of teaching and how challenging classroom situations encouraged her to reconsider her understanding of effective teaching. Initially, the interviewee believed that successful teaching depended on carefully planned lessons, classroom management, academic performance, and maintaining the image of a perfect teacher. However, when she experienced difficulties engaging students, she began questioning her abilities and professional assumptions. An unexpected conversation with a quiet student became a turning point that helped her recognize the importance of relationships, empathy, student confidence, and meaningful learning experiences.

I selected this artifact because it demonstrates how educators can learn from authentic experiences, critically examine their instructional approaches, and develop more effective ways of supporting diverse learners. The interview illustrates a transition from teacher-centered instruction toward a more student-centered learning environment in which students are encouraged to participate, communicate, express ideas, and take risks without fear of making mistakes.

The instructional approaches discussed in the interview include reflective practice, learner-centered instruction, supportive classroom communication, emotional engagement, and adapting teaching strategies to students' individual needs. These approaches demonstrate that effective instructional design requires educators to consider not only what students learn but also how they experience the learning process. Dewey (1933) emphasizes the importance of reflective thinking in learning from experience, while Schön (1983) explains how professionals improve their practice through reflection and the reconsideration of their actions.

Although the interview does not directly document the use of digital instructional tools, it provides a foundation for understanding how technology can support transformative learning. For example, digital collaboration platforms, interactive learning activities, online discussion spaces, and multimedia resources can create additional opportunities for students to communicate, collaborate, reflect, and develop new perspectives. The integration of technology should be guided by instructional objectives and learners' needs rather than simply introducing digital tools into the classroom. This understanding is consistent with the importance of connecting instructional approaches with meaningful learning experiences and professional reflection.

Overall, this artifact demonstrates my developing understanding of how reflective teaching, creative instructional strategies, and learner-centered practices contribute to meaningful educational experiences. It also provides an opportunity to consider how educational technologies can be used intentionally to support collaboration, student engagement, and transformative learning.

2 So What? (Transformative Learning Impact & Theory)

Analyzing this interview helped me understand that transformative learning involves more than acquiring new knowledge or developing additional skills. It requires individuals to question existing assumptions, examine their experiences, and develop new ways of understanding themselves and the world around them. The interview strongly connects to Mezirow's (1997) transformative learning theory, which emphasizes critical reflection and the development of new perspectives. The teacher's challenging classroom experience represents a disorienting dilemma because it challenged her previous beliefs about perfection, classroom control, and professional success.

Kitchenham (2008) explains the evolution of Mezirow's transformative learning theory and the importance of critical reflection in changing established perspectives. This connection is evident when the interviewee begins questioning why she associates student engagement with her personal worth as a teacher and why she believes successful instruction must always follow a perfect plan. Instead of continuing to view classroom challenges as personal failures, she begins recognizing them as opportunities for reflection, adaptation, and professional development.

Cranton (2016) also emphasizes the importance of examining assumptions and supporting perspective transformation through meaningful learning experiences. The interviewee demonstrates this process by reconsidering her beliefs and developing a more flexible understanding of her professional responsibilities. Similarly, Brookfield (2017) explains that critically reflective educators examine the assumptions that influence their instructional decisions. This helped me recognize that educators should continuously evaluate their teaching approaches rather than assume that one instructional method will be effective for every learner.

The interview also demonstrates the relationship between transformative learning and creativity. Creativity in education is not limited to developing new technologies or designing visually attractive classroom activities. It also involves identifying problems, considering alternative perspectives, and developing new approaches to existing challenges. The teacher's movement from perfection-oriented teaching toward a more flexible, empathetic, and learner-centered approach illustrates this type of creative problem-solving. Freire (2000) emphasizes the importance of critical consciousness and meaningful participation in education, reinforcing the idea that learners should be active participants rather than passive recipients of information.

Furthermore, Taylor (1997) examines the theoretical and empirical development of transformative learning and provides an important foundation for understanding how individuals experience changes in their perspectives. This supports my interpretation of the interview as an example of professional transformation through challenging experiences, reflection, and changes in behavior.

The interview also connects to the OECD Learning Compass 2030, which emphasizes competencies such as creating new value, reconciling tensions and dilemmas, and taking responsibility (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development [OECD], 2019). The teacher demonstrates these competencies by reconsidering her instructional assumptions, responding creatively to classroom challenges, and taking responsibility for improving her professional practice. Her experience demonstrates that innovation can begin when educators recognize that traditional approaches are not meeting learners' needs and become willing to explore alternative solutions.

One of the most meaningful aspects of the interview is the impact of the teacher's transformation on her students. After changing her teaching philosophy, she noticed that students became more comfortable expressing their ideas, participating in classroom activities, and taking risks. This demonstrates how an educator's professional growth can contribute to a more inclusive and supportive learning environment. Mezirow (1991) explains that transformative learning involves changes in how individuals interpret their experiences, while the interview illustrates how these changes can influence professional decisions and relationships with others.

This artifact directly supports MA EDIN Program Learning Outcome 2 because it demonstrates the importance of reflective thinking, adaptive instructional practices, and creating learning environments that encourage meaningful participation and personal development. It helped me recognize that transformative education is not simply about delivering information more effectively. It is about creating opportunities for learners to develop confidence, question assumptions, solve problems, and apply new understanding to real-life situations.

3 Now What? (Scaling & Iterating Future Practice)

Moving forward, I intend to apply the lessons from this interview to my future educational and professional practice. One of the most important insights I gained is that effective educators must remain open to learning, reflection, feedback, and change. Teaching should not be viewed as a fixed process in which educators simply deliver information. Instead, it should be understood as an ongoing process of evaluating learners' needs, adapting instructional strategies, and creating meaningful learning opportunities.

In my future practice, I want to incorporate more learner-centered instructional strategies that encourage collaboration, critical thinking, creativity, and active participation. I also want to create supportive learning environments in which students feel comfortable expressing their opinions, asking questions, making mistakes, and exploring different solutions. The interview reminded me that students' confidence and sense of belonging can influence their willingness to participate and take responsibility for their learning.

I also plan to integrate educational technologies more intentionally into instructional design. Digital learning platforms, collaborative tools, multimedia resources, and interactive activities can provide opportunities for students to engage with content in different ways. However, I understand that technology alone does not create transformative learning. Its effectiveness depends on how well it supports instructional goals, encourages meaningful participation, and responds to learners' needs.

Dewey's (1933) emphasis on reflective thinking will encourage me to evaluate my instructional experiences and identify opportunities for improvement. Schön's (1983) reflective practitioner approach will also influence how I examine challenges during and after instructional activities. Rather than viewing unsuccessful lessons as failures, I will use them as opportunities to reconsider my decisions and develop more effective teaching strategies.

In addition, I want to continue developing my ability to recognize diverse perspectives and respond to different learning needs. The interview demonstrated that even a brief interaction with a student can challenge an educator's assumptions and create an opportunity for meaningful change. Brookfield (2017) reinforces the importance of critically examining professional assumptions, while Cranton (2016) highlights the value of supporting transformative experiences through intentional educational practice.

Ultimately, I hope to develop instructional approaches that combine creativity, reflective thinking, appropriate technology, and collaboration. I will continue evaluating the effectiveness of these approaches through student feedback, participation, reflection, and learning outcomes. This will allow me to refine and adapt my instructional practices rather than rely on methods simply because they are familiar.

This interview has strengthened my understanding that educational innovation begins with a willingness to question existing practices and consider new possibilities. It has also reinforced my belief that educators have an important responsibility to create learning environments where students can develop academically, socially, and personally. By applying these lessons, I aim to contribute to more meaningful, inclusive, and transformative learning experiences and demonstrate continued growth toward MA EDIN Program Learning Outcome 2.
